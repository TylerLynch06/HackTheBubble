Tech giant unveils interface made entirely of water

By Staff Reporter · 14 November · 4 min read

In a keynote watched by millions, executives revealed a bold new design language which they say "removes the barrier between the user and the content", mostly by removing the content.

Early testers reported that buttons now drift gently away from the cursor, text slowly slides to the bottom of the screen, and the whole window fills with water if left unattended for more than a minute. A spokesperson described this as "working as intended".

Accessibility advocates raised concerns about contrast, legibility and the general wetness of the experience. The company responded by making the glass thicker. "We hear you," said the spokesperson, through several layers of frosted blur.

Analysts expect the update to reach most devices by spring, at which point users will be offered a single setting labelled "Reduce Transparency". Pressing it is understood to increase transparency.

Asked whether users could simply turn the feature off, the spokesperson smiled, gestured at a slide reading Courage, and walked into the sea.
